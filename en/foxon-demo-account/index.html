With Fox On, you get R$10,000 in virtual balance on the demo account to test the platform until you feel confident enough to trade. It's the same environment as the live account, with the same charts and assets, just without risking your own money.

In this guide, you'll learn the difference between a demo and a live account, how long it's worth staying on the demo, and how to make the switch to a live account the right way.

Demo account vs. live account: the short answer

The demo account uses virtual money. It's ideal for learning, testing strategies and practicing with no financial risk.

The live account uses your own money. That's where emotional pressure, greater care with risk and responsibility for every decision come in.

The market is the same in both. What changes is how you behave when the result actually counts. That's why it's worth using both, each for its own purpose.

1. The demo is for learning the platform

The point of the demo account isn't to make you feel profitable — it's to clear up the confusion of the early days. Before using real money, you should be able to do the following without much thought:

  • Open and close a trade;
  • Switch the chart timeframe;
  • Add and remove indicators;
  • Understand how the trade size changes how much you can lose;
  • Check your trade history;
  • Explain, in one sentence, why you opened each trade.

2. On a live account, decision quality is what counts

With money on the line, the question stops being "can I find an opportunity?" and becomes "can I stick to my plan even under pressure?". It's common to wait patiently on the demo and then enter too early on the live account, or to accept a loss on the demo and move the stop on the live account. Practicing discipline on the demo helps avoid that.

3. A winning streak on the demo can be misleading

Winning several times in a row on the demo is a good sign, but it doesn't prove the strategy works. To trust a result, look at dozens of trades following the same rules, not just the last three.

4. How long should you stay on the demo account?

There's no fixed timeframe. You're ready for a live account when:

  • You use the platform without hesitation;
  • You follow a plan with clear rules for entry, exit and trade size;
  • Your trade history shows consistency, not luck;
  • You know how much you're willing to lose before opening each trade.

5. How to move from the demo to a live account

  1. Use the same strategy: don't switch methods right when you start using real money.
  2. Start with small amounts: enough to feel the weight of the decision, without straining your budget.
  3. Set your risk in advance: use a stop-loss and never risk more than 1% to 2% of your balance per trade.
  4. Keep using the demo: test new ideas there before taking them to the live account.

What the demo doesn't teach you

The demo doesn't reproduce the fear of losing or the anxiety of trying to win back a loss. Those feelings only show up with real money, which is why the transition should be made gradually, with small amounts and clear rules.

Final thoughts

The demo account trains the technique; the live account tests the behavior. Use Fox On's R$10,000 in virtual funds to master the platform and your strategy, and only then move on to the live account, calmly and with risk under control.
